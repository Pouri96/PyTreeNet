- Our method, for reference: TEBD/MPS, two-site SVD cut, chi kept fixed, kept subspace tilted by one Gauss-Newton step (Q = qr(U_k + U_perp C)) so that the 1-, 2- and 3-site RDMs of a small region match those of the untruncated tensor. ## 1. Direct prior art inside tensor-network dynamics (largest prior-art risk)

### Takeaway
The principle "truncate so that local observables / few-site RDMs are preserved rather than minimizing the Frobenius error" is already established for **mixed-state (MPDO) TEBD**: Density Matrix Truncation (DMT, 2017) preserves all 3-site RDMs exactly, and reweighted TEBD (rTEBD, 2024) biases the SVD toward low-Pauli-weight operators. Our method looks new only as a **pure-state, fixed-chi, tangent-space tilt** version of this idea. The novelty claim has to be framed against DMT and rTEBD explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- DMT (White, Zaletel, Mong, Refael, "Quantum dynamics of thermalizing systems", arXiv:1707.01506) works on matrix-product density operators. Its truncation "ameliorates the positivity problem of Frobenius truncation while exactly preserving expectation values of operators on regions of up to three sites", so the 3-site RDMs are unchanged by truncation — [arXiv:1707.01506](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.01506) (snippet level)
- A follow-up generalizes DMT to a preservation diameter ℓ. It splits the bond dimension into χ^(preserve) (stated as 2^ℓ in the snippet), which stores all observables on ℓ contiguous sites around the cut, and χ^(extra), which keeps the largest remaining correlations. It uses a basis transformation before the SVD. The paper is "Emergent hydrodynamics in non-equilibrium quantum systems" — [arXiv:1902.01859](https://arxiv.org/html/1902.01859) (snippet level; I did not verify whether 2^ℓ or 4^ℓ applies to the operator space)
- DMT is motivated by the observation that Frobenius-optimal SVD truncation "is blind to the fact that some operators, especially local operators like energy density, are more important than others". By preserving energy density, magnetization and their currents, it captures late-time hydrodynamics. Its early-time dynamics are only approximate — [arXiv:1902.01859](https://arxiv.org/html/1902.01859) (snippet level)
- rTEBD (Guha Roy and Slagle, Rice) reweights the MPDO in a reweighted Pauli basis. Correlators with n Pauli operators are suppressed by γ^(-n) during the SVD truncation, which "causes the SVD truncation step to more accurately preserve the expectation value of few-body operators". The method only needs some matrices rescaled by γ. It is reported as "significantly more accurate than standard TEBD time evolution of an MPDO, and competitive with, and sometimes better than, TEBD using MPS", and it preserves conserved quantities to high precision. Code: github guharoysayak/reweighted_TEBD. Submitted to SciPost — [arXiv:2412.08730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.08730); [SciPost submission](https://scipost.org/submissions/2412.08730v3) (abstract level)
- The MPS time-evolution review by Paeckel et al. already notes that truncation "based on the left/right density matrix or by optimizing the local density matrix aims at preserving local observables" — [arXiv:1901.05824](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.05824) (snippet level)
- A benchmark on an infinite-temperature 1D lattice spin model compared TEBD, TEBD+DMT, the recursion method (R-UOG) and operator-size truncation (OST). DMT and OST give consistent correlations out to t = 60/J, with diffusion constants within 1% of each other. Plain TEBD converges only to t ≈ 20, but its diffusion constants are still within 1%. R-UOG fails to converge. The paper also reports "hot band second sound" in the energy-density correlator — Yi-Thomas, Ware, Sau, White, [arXiv:2310.06886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06886), PRB 110, 134308 (doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.110.134308) (abstract level)
- Fermionic DAOE and DMT agree on diffusion constants in weakly interacting chains, consistent with D ∼ U^4 — [arXiv:2311.17148](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.17148) (snippet level)
- A 2026 tensor-ring paper reduces a weighted (metric-aware) truncation to an ordinary SVD when the metric is separable, and uses ALS otherwise. This is the same separable-vs-general split as in weighted low-rank theory (Section 3) — [arXiv:2610.10027](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10027) (title/snippet level)

### Inferences
- Insertion point and distinction. DMT and rTEBD act in **operator space** (MPDOs). There the density matrix has enough freedom to keep all 3-site marginals exactly at modest χ. A **pure-state** rank-χ MPS generally cannot match all neighbouring RDMs exactly, which is why our method is a least-squares (Gauss-Newton) tilt rather than an exact constraint. That gives a clean novelty statement: "a DMT-like objective for pure-state MPS at fixed χ, implemented as a first-order tangent-space correction to SVD." Prior-art risk: **high for the principle, moderate for the pure-state tangent-tilt realization.** I found no pure-state MPS method that tilts the SVD subspace to match local RDMs, but the search was not exhaustive.
- Idea to borrow from rTEBD: replace the explicit RDM-matching step with a **reweighting of the matrix before an SVD** (a whitening). If the local-RDM objective can be approximated by a separable metric (left environment ⊗ right environment, or a Pauli-weight reweighting), the optimum is a single SVD of the reweighted matrix at about SVD cost, instead of 3-16x. The tensor-ring result above is the same mechanism.
- Heisenberg failure versus DMT. DMT and rTEBD emphasize exact preservation of **conserved densities and currents**. The Heisenberg chain has a U(1) charge with slow hydrodynamic modes. One plausible reading is that the local-RDM targets drift through slow long-wavelength modes that no single-cut correction can repair. A test: add the conserved-charge current on the cut bond to the target set, which mimics what DMT keeps (energy and charge densities plus currents). This is speculation.

### Gaps
- I could not read the DMT construction or the rTEBD benchmark tables in full, so the quantitative comparison with our 2.5-5x is open. The key question is whether rTEBD on pure-state-like MPDOs reaches similar local-error gains at equal χ.
- I could not check whether any 2019-2026 paper does "variationally optimize the local density matrix" for pure MPS. Paeckel et al. hint at such schemes, but the snippet does not name them.

## 2. Machine-learning model compression (data/activation-aware SVD, Fisher weighting, whitening, LoRA)

### Takeaway
LLM compression has met exactly our problem: SVD is Frobenius-optimal on the weights but not on the **outputs on the calibration distribution**. The standard fixes are (i) closed-form **whitening** (SVD of W·S with S from a Cholesky factor of the activation covariance), which is provably optimal for the output-reconstruction objective, (ii) **row-wise Fisher scaling** (closed form) versus **elementwise Fisher weighting** (iterative, non-convex, better but slower), and (iii) a **post-truncation update of the remaining weights**. The known failure mode is calibration overfitting: good in-distribution perplexity alongside lost knowledge or OOD accuracy. That is a close analog of our "near vs far observables" collateral cost.

### Cited Findings
- **SVD-LLM** (Wang, Zheng, Wan, Zhang; ICLR 2025) points out two weaknesses of earlier SVD compression: truncating the smallest singular values can still give larger loss than expected, and the remaining weights are not updated afterwards. It builds a whitening matrix S by Cholesky decomposition of calibration activation statistics, then truncates the SVD of W·S. A theoretical proof gives "a direct mapping between singular values and model compression loss". At high compression it adds a layer-wise closed-form update of the remaining weights. It was evaluated on 10-11 datasets and 7 models from 3 LLM families — [arXiv:2403.07378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07378) (abstract level)
- SVD-LLM V2 ("Optimizing singular value truncation") is the follow-up — [arXiv:2503.12340](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2503.12340) (title level only)
- **ASVD** (activation-aware SVD) scales the weight matrix column-wise with a diagonal S built from mean absolute activations, s_i = (mean_j |X_ji|)^α with α ≈ 0.5. It truncates the SVD of W·S and folds S^{-1} back: W ≈ U_k Σ_k (V_k^T S^{-1}). This helps most for channels with outlier activations — [arXiv:2312.05821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.05821) (snippet level). Dobi-SVD reports that ASVD's inverse scaling "often fails due to theoretical and numerical issues" — [arXiv:2502.02723](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.02723) (snippet level)
- **FWSVD** (Hsu et al., ICLR 2022) weights parameters by Fisher information, which measures their effect on the task prediction. It uses a simplified importance with **one weight per row**, so the problem reduces to an SVD of a pre-scaled matrix (closed form). Its limits: it needs a task objective and task data, and the row-wise simplification "does not fully utilize the Fisher information" — [arXiv:2207.00112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00112) (abstract level)
- **TFWSVD** (Hua et al., EMNLP 2022) uses an elementwise empirical-Fisher weight. Weighted decomposition is "a non-convex optimization problem that lacks a closed-form solution", so the authors use a hybrid Adam→SGD optimizer. They introduce a **"Fisher information variance" metric that predicts when standard SVD will fail**. TFWSVD beats FWSVD, TVD and SVD on GLUE and CoNLL, at higher compute cost — [arXiv:2211.09718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09718); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.91.pdf) (abstract level)
- Generalized Fisher-Weighted SVD (Kronecker-factored Fisher, using off-diagonal Fisher terms) is reported as an ICLR 2026 submission under review — [secondary copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/1007455222/Generalized-Fisher-Weighted-SVD-Scalable-Kronecker-Factored-Fisher-Approximation-for-Compressing-Large-Language-Models) (unvetted; title/snippet level)
- **CorDA** (NeurIPS 2024) takes the SVD of W·C, where C is the input-activation covariance, and rebuilds Ŵ = UΣV^T C^{-1}, so the model output is unchanged at initialization. In "knowledge-preserved" mode the smallest components initialize the adapter and the large ones stay frozen, which reduces world-knowledge forgetting relative to LoRA. CorDA++ adds dynamic covariance selection and rank allocation — [arXiv:2406.05223](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05223); [code](https://github.com/iboing/CorDA); [CorDA++ arXiv:2506.13187](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13187) (abstract level)
- **GPTQ/OBQ/OBS** quantize or prune weights sequentially. After each step the error is compensated on the not-yet-processed weights using the inverse Hessian H = XX^T: w_j ← w_j − e_j [H^{-1}]_{ji}/[H^{-1}]_{ii}. A damping λI is added to XX^T for stability, and a Cholesky reformulation plus lazy batching make it scale. Processing order has minimal effect — [GPTQ arXiv:2210.17323](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.17323) (snippet level)
- **Calibration overfitting / OOD:** NSVD says FWSVD, ASVD and SVD-LLM "still suffer from performance degradation at medium to high compression ratios or datasets with significantly different activations (i.e., 'overfitting' happens during the post-training procedure)" — [arXiv:2503.17101](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17101) (snippet level)
- **Perplexity hides knowledge loss.** In one model study (Mistral-7B, plain SVD at 50% rank), WikiText perplexity stays reasonable while MMLU drops from 62.3% to 51.5% and LAMA from 54.1% to 42.8%. These are the authors' own numbers, used to motivate their "Fisher-aligned subspace" method — [arXiv:2601.07197](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07197) (snippet level). LLM-KICK finds that pruning methods degrade badly on knowledge-intensive tasks even at 25-30% sparsity — [ICLR 2024 / Apple ML](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/compressing-llms) (abstract level)
- Perplexity is a weak predictor of compression damage (best overall leave-one-out correlation 0.093). A spectral statistic, compression ratio × stable rank, predicts it with r ≈ 0.84-0.89 — [arXiv:2604.18085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18085) (abstract level)
- Whitening makes the calibration distribution decide directly which singular directions are kept, and these methods still lean on fine-tuning to recover accuracy — [arXiv:2608.08506](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.08506) (snippet level)
- Further 2026 variants: AA-SVD "anchored and adaptive" ([arXiv:2604.02119](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.02119)), Zero-Sum SVD ([arXiv:2602.02848](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02848v1)), LACE-SVD with cumulative error correction ([arXiv:2607.03057](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03057v1)), AdaSVD ([arXiv:2502.01403](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01403)), Low-Rank Prehab ([arXiv:2512.01980](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01980)), and SAFE-SVD for physics foundation models ([arXiv:2605.17985](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17985)) — (title level only; I do not know their mechanisms)

### Inferences
- **Mathematical correspondence.** Our objective, "match the region's RDMs computed from the untruncated tensor", is an output-reconstruction objective. The "outputs" are the local marginals and the "inputs" are the neighbouring tensors. In ML, when the objective is a quadratic ‖(W − Ŵ)X‖_F, the optimum is **closed form**: whiten with S = chol(XX^T), take the SVD of WS, then un-whiten. The analog for us is to linearize the RDM map around the SVD solution, form the Gauss-Newton metric J^T J, and, if it is (approximately) Kronecker-separable over the left/right bond index, whiten by its square root and take one SVD. This could replace iteration-like cost with about one extra SVD plus a small Cholesky. If J^T J is not separable we are in the elementwise TFWSVD regime, which has no closed form (Section 3).
- **Handling collateral cost: three ML tricks that transfer.**
  1. *Damping or anchoring.* GPTQ's λI damping and AA-SVD's "anchoring" both cap how far the solution moves from the reference. For us that means a Levenberg-Marquardt step on C, or an objective = local-RDM error + μ·(Frobenius/fidelity error). Varying μ interpolates SVD ↔ tilt and maps a Pareto front of near- versus far-observable error.
  2. *A diagnostic that gates the expensive step.* TFWSVD's "Fisher information variance" predicts when SVD fails. Our analog is a cheap per-cut statistic, e.g. the targeted RDM error of SVD relative to the norm of the first-order collateral (‖J_far δ‖), used to tilt only where it pays. This attacks the 3-16x cost directly, and it matches the mechanism already identified: the tilt pays only when SVD's targeted error is large compared with the collateral.
  3. *Compensation on what is not yet truncated.* GPTQ, SVD-LLM's layer-wise update and LACE-SVD's cumulative correction push errors onto later, not-yet-processed parameters. In TEBD the analog is to carry the tilt's residual RDM mismatch into the next cut of the sweep (error feedback) rather than repairing each cut in isolation.
- **Near vs far = in-distribution vs OOD.** The ML evidence (NSVD, the knowledge-loss studies) is that objective-aware truncation overfits to the calibration set and pays elsewhere. That supports our finding that the tilt has a first-order collateral cost, and it argues for *reporting far observables and fidelity next to the targeted RDMs*. The ML field learned that one headline metric (perplexity) hides the collateral.
- Prior-art risk in ML: none for TEBD specifically. The whitening and Fisher ideas are generic and should be cited as inspiration.

### Gaps
- I did not verify any tensorized-network results (TT/MPO layers: Novikov et al. "Tensorizing neural networks"; CompactifAI). See the unverified leads below.
- I found no ML paper that explicitly quantifies "targeted-output gain vs collateral OOD loss" as a first-order trade-off with theory. The ML evidence is empirical.

## 3. Weighted / generalized low-rank approximation theory and Riemannian fixed-rank methods

### Takeaway
Eckart-Young (SVD optimality) survives only when the weights are **separable (rank-one weight matrix) or two-sided** (‖Q₁^{1/2}(A−X)Q₂^{1/2}‖), and Friedland-Torokhti give the generalized closed form for min ‖A − BXC‖. General elementwise weights give a non-convex problem with no closed form, NP-hard (Gillis-Glineur), with only local-minimum algorithms. Our one-step Gauss-Newton tilt with QR is precisely a Riemannian (tangent-space step + retraction) first iteration on the fixed-rank / Grassmann manifold, started from the SVD.

### Cited Findings
- Srebro and Jaakkola (ICML 2003): weighted low-rank problems "do not admit a closed form solution in general". Only the rank-one weight case (row weights × column weights) "can be solved directly, analogously to SVD". They give an EM algorithm — [ICML 2003 slides](https://home.ttic.edu/~nati/Publications/ICML03slides.pdf); [AAAI/ICML entry](https://aaai.org/papers/icml03-094-weighted-low-rank-approximations/) (abstract level)
- Manton, Mahony and Hua, "The geometry of weighted low-rank approximations" (IEEE TSP 2003): the problem reduces to finding the subspace that minimizes a particular cost (a Grassmann formulation). There is no closed form in general, and existing algorithms converge only to local minima — [eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt08m9c4z6/qt08m9c4z6.pdf?t=lnotos) (snippet level)
- Friedland-Torokhti (SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 2007) give a closed form for min_{rank X ≤ r} ‖A − BXC‖_F, X = B†(P_B A P_C)_r C†, which generalizes Eckart-Young. Later work qualifies the minimal-norm property and the behaviour in infinite dimensions — [arXiv:2408.05104](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.05104) (snippet level)
- Gillis-Glineur: weighted low-rank approximation is NP-hard in general — [cited in search summary; arXiv:1012.0197 per the task brief] (not re-read)
- Algorithmic weighted-LRA literature: reweighted solutions with guarantees ([arXiv:2406.02431](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02431)); Riemannian SGD for weighted LRA ([arXiv:2502.14174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14174)); acceleration ([arXiv:2109.11057](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.11057)); additive-error guarantees ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10337226)) — (title/snippet level)
- Riemannian fixed-rank geometry. Rank-r matrices form a smooth manifold. Its tangent space consists of first-order perturbations of the column and row spaces (rank ≤ 2r), and truncated SVD of X + Z is a valid (metric-projection) retraction. Vandereycken's Riemannian CG for matrix completion uses metric projection as the retraction and vector transport for CG directions — [arXiv:1209.3834](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1209.3834), SIAM J. Optim. 23(2):1214-1236, doi:10.1137/110845768 (abstract level)
- Absil and Oseledets, "Low-rank retractions: a survey and new results" (Comput. Optim. Appl. 62:5-29, 2015). Apart from the exponential map, the retractions differ little in flops. They differ in domain, boundedness, first- versus second-order accuracy and symmetry — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/coopap/v62y2015i1p5-29.html) (abstract level)
- Uschmajew and Vandereycken, "Geometric methods on low-rank matrix and tensor manifolds" (chapter covering fixed-rank, tangent space, retraction, TT manifolds) — [PDF](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/103122/978-3-030-31351-7_9.pdf) (snippet level)
- Séguin, Ceruti and Kressner link low-rank retractions to dynamical low-rank approximation (projector-splitting integrators are retractions and vice versa) — [arXiv:2309.06125](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.06125) (title/snippet level)

### Inferences
- Our Q = qr(U_k + U_perp C) is a **QR-based retraction on the Grassmannian** of the tangent step (U_perp C is a horizontal tangent vector at U_k). One Gauss-Newton step from the SVD point is therefore "one Riemannian Gauss-Newton iteration for a weighted LRA, initialized at the unweighted optimum". By Manton et al., the true weighted optimum is a subspace-optimization problem with local minima. So the method is local by design, and its first-order gain/collateral trade-off is the standard first-order expansion of the weighted cost around the Frobenius optimum.
- **When the closed form applies.** If the local-marginal objective can be written (after linearization) as ‖L(A − X)R‖ with L and R acting on the left and right bond spaces, the Friedland-Torokhti / two-sided-whitening closed form is the exact optimum: an SVD of L A R, then un-whiten. This is the theoretical basis for the "whitened SVD" alternative suggested in Sections 1-2, and the separable-metric reduction in the tensor-ring paper [arXiv:2610.10027](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10027). Elementwise or non-separable dependence (the full RDM map is quadratic in the state) puts us back in the NP-hard regime. That justifies a local first-order method there.
- Theory on collateral error. I found no general theorem bounding "error on unweighted entries" as a function of the weighted gain. One can be sketched: at the Frobenius optimum the gradient of the Frobenius loss vanishes on the tangent space, so a tangent step δ costs O(‖δ‖²) in Frobenius/fidelity (second order) but can change any *particular* non-targeted observable at first order. This matches "fidelity unchanged" alongside first-order collateral on far observables. (My own derivation; it should be checked.)

### Gaps
- I could not access the full texts of Manton et al., Friedland-Torokhti, Srebro-Jaakkola or Absil-Oseledets, so exact optimality conditions are second-hand.
- I found no source treating weighted LRA where the weight is defined *implicitly* through a quadratic (RDM) map of the factors. That is our setting.

## 4. Quantum chemistry: DMRG, RDMs, DMRG-SCF, NEVPT2

### Takeaway
In ab initio DMRG, 1-, 2-, 3- and 4-RDMs are the products that feed DMRG-SCF (orbital gradients) and NEVPT2/CASPT2. In practice the high-order RDMs are computed from a **compressed, lower-bond-dimension MPS**, because 4-RDM cost is the bottleneck. I found **no DMRG truncation that targets RDM accuracy** rather than discarded weight; truncation is by discarded weight or fixed D (DBSS). That makes "RDM-targeted compression of the reference MPS before computing 3/4-RDMs" a concrete, apparently open insertion point. The caveats: chemistry RDMs are long-range (orbital indices are non-local along the chain), and energy errors that are first-order in the RDMs feed directly into NEVPT2 denominators.

### Cited Findings
- DMRG-SC-NEVPT2 (Guo, Watson, Hu, Sun, Chan): "to minimize the cost, it is generally necessary to evaluate the 4-RDM using a lower bond dimension than is used in the DMRG energy optimization". This is done with a "reverse schedule" set of sweeps that decreases M, and the 4-RDM is computed on the fly — [arXiv:1512.08137](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08137) (snippet level)
- In a hydrogen-chain benchmark, compressing the DMRG-CASSCF wavefunction to D = 500 gave total-energy compression errors below 0.3 mEh at most geometries but **10 mEh at the shortest bond length** — [arXiv:1705.01608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.01608) (snippet level)
- Standard DMRG-NEVPT2 "is not feasible for large-scale DMRG calculations with more than 24 orbitals due to the cost of computing the 4-particle density matrix". The projected DMRG-SC-NEVPT2 approximation targets "moderate and controllable accuracy" — Roemelt, Guo, Chan, [JCP 144, 204113 (2016)](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jcp/article/144/20/204113/912413/A-projected-approximation-to-strongly-contracted-N) (snippet level). Cholesky-based DMRG-MRPT is in [arXiv:1608.02006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.02006) / [JCTC doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.6b00778](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jctc.6b00778) (snippet level)
- Cumulant approximations to the 3/4-RDM (Cu4, Cu34; e.g. in ORCA) have an effect that is "limited for simpler cases (such as stilbene photoisomerization) but profound or even catastrophic for highly multireference systems, such as stretched N₂ or Cr₂". A filtering scheme gave SC-NEVPT2 errors **up to 2.5 Ha**. Approximated cumulants can create "false intruders" through the density dependence of the Koopmans matrices in the denominators (Zgid et al.) — [ORCA 6.1 NEVPT2 manual](https://www.faccts.de/docs/orca/6.1/manual/contents/modelchemistries/NEVPT2.html); [arXiv:1512.08137](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.08137) (snippet level). In DMRG-PC-NEVPT2 the anomalous norms are attributed to false intruders, while SC-NEVPT2 norms stay at 0.5-0.6 regardless of m — [JCTC doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.6b00778](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jctc.6b00778) (snippet level)
- DMRG-SCF stability: "only if the error of the DMRG solution is kept below the error settings of the SCF procedure could the DMRG-SCF procedure lead to stable convergence". Optimized orbitals of iron-sulfur clusters depend more sensitively on DMRG parameters than those of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons. Low-D runs (D = 16-64) are used to pick orbital orderings, and orbital optimization lowers the D needed for a given accuracy — [arXiv:2503.20700](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20700) / [JCTC doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.5c00571](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c00571) (snippet level); review: Baiardi and Reiher, [arXiv:1910.00137](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.00137), JCP 152, 040903 (snippet level)
- DMRG truncation in chemistry keeps the largest reduced-density-matrix eigenvalues (discarded weight). Energy error is roughly linear in discarded weight and is used for extrapolation, though the linearity fails in momentum-space DMRG. The alternative selection is DBSS (fixed truncation error) — [arXiv:1910.00137](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.00137); [cond-mat/0110420](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0110420); [ML-boosted DMRG, JPCL doi:10.1021/acs.jpclett.5c00207](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jpclett.5c00207) (snippet level)
- Fermionic mode (orbital) optimization compresses multireference character, an alternative route to a lower χ for a given accuracy — [arXiv:2204.13531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.13531) (title level). QCMaquis 4.0 is a current production DMRG code with NEVPT2/vibrational features — [arXiv:2505.01405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.01405) (title level)

### Inferences
- **Best insertion point: the "reverse schedule" compression of the converged reference MPS before computing 3/4-RDMs for NEVPT2.** This is a pure compression (no time evolution), done at the step where accuracy of the RDMs (not fidelity) is what matters, and where errors as large as 10 mEh at D = 500 have been reported. An RDM-targeted tilt at each cut of that compression sweep is a direct transplant. The open question is whether the targeted RDM elements can be made "local around the cut" — in an orbital-ordered MPS, the 2-RDM elements Γ_pqrs with p,q,r,s near the cut are local, but most are not.
- **DMRG-SCF.** Orbital gradients depend linearly on the 1- and 2-RDM. A truncation that lowers 2-RDM error at fixed D could stabilize macro-iterations at lower D, which matters most for Fe-S-like systems whose orbitals are sensitive to DMRG parameters. Expected benefit is moderate; there is a risk that collateral error in non-targeted (long-range) RDM elements hurts the orbital rotation.
- **NEVPT2 false intruders** are the chemistry analog of "collateral cost": an approximate higher RDM that is accurate on average but inconsistent can be catastrophic. Any RDM-targeted truncation must keep N-representability-like consistency. Because our tilt produces a genuine pure state, its RDMs are automatically consistent (N-representable), unlike cumulant approximations. That is a real selling point over Cu4.
- Tensor hypercontraction (THC) factorizes integrals, not the wavefunction. Its analogy is only to "objective-aware factorization", not to RDM-targeted truncation.
- Prior-art risk in chemistry: low based on what I found, but I searched only one pass.

### Gaps
- I found no study quantifying 1-/2-RDM error (as opposed to energy) versus D in DMRG-SCF, or how 2-RDM error propagates into DMRG-SCF convergence.
- I could not verify the Kurashige-Yanai cumulant-NEVPT2/CASPT2 papers or the Zgid et al. 2009 cumulant study directly (only the secondary mention).
- I did not check whether the "projected DMRG-SC-NEVPT2" or "DMRG-PT" (perturbative DMRG) papers include any truncation targeting RDM accuracy.

## 5. Physics-exploration use cases: quantum-simulator benchmarking and thermalization

### Takeaway
Quantum-advantage benchmarking in 2023-2026 is overwhelmingly about **local observables** (single-site magnetization, two-point ZZ correlations, diffusion constants), not fidelity. MPS is often the reference baseline whose cost sets the claimed classical hardness (D-Wave's "million years on Frontier"). Most refutations used lattice-geometry TNs with belief propagation, t-VMC or Pauli propagation, not better MPS. A 2-5x local-error reduction at equal χ during entanglement growth would directly lower the χ needed to match QPU accuracy, and so the MPS cost estimates these claims quote. It would not overturn 2D-native-method arguments.

### Cited Findings
- **IBM "utility" kicked Ising** (Kim et al., Nature 618, 500 (2023)): 127 qubits, 5 Trotter steps in the verifiable regime. Tindall, Fishman, Stoudenmire and Sels, using a heavy-hex TN with belief propagation, reach about 10^-14 accuracy for average magnetization at 5 steps. BP and boundary-MPS agree closely for single-site observables, and the MPS error grows from about step 10 — [arXiv:2306.14887](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.14887), PRX Quantum 5, 010308 (doi:10.1103/PRXQuantum.5.010308) (abstract level). Begušić, Gray and Chan's sparse Pauli dynamics + mixed Schrödinger/Heisenberg TN simulate the observables orders of magnitude faster than the experiment and converge beyond experimental accuracy — [arXiv:2308.05077](https://awesomepapers.io/quantum-computing/papers/2308.05077), Sci. Adv. 10, eadk4321; also [arXiv:2306.16372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16372). PEPO (Heisenberg picture): [arXiv:2308.03082](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03082v1). Graph-PEPS up to 1121 qubits: [arXiv:2309.15642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.15642). Anand, Temme, Kandala and Zaletel, MPS benchmarking of ZNE beyond the verifiable regime: [arXiv:2306.17839] (title only; abstract not retrieved) (abstract level unless stated)
- **D-Wave "Beyond-classical computation in quantum simulation"** (King et al., Science 2025; [arXiv:2403.00910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.00910)): about 5000 qubits; square, cubic, diamond and biclique lattices; target observable two-point ZZ correlations. They estimated that MPS would need nearly a million years on Frontier to match QPU quality. Tindall et al.'s rebuttal ([arXiv:2503.05693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05693); a Science 2026 publication is reported but not verified) uses lattice-matched BP-TNs and reports lower error than the QPU in two cases. They note the scaling analysis "applies to MPS — the only method with which we can match QPU quality for all considered quench times — and is not an intrinsic lower bound for all classical methods". Mauron and Carleo's t-VMC with Jastrow-Feenberg wavefunctions keeps correlation errors below 7% up to 128 spins on 3D diamond — [arXiv:2503.08247](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.08247). A 2026 t-VMC preprint covers all four topologies, including biclique — [arXiv:2609.01719](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01719). There is also a "Comment on Beyond-classical…" — [arXiv:2607.08811](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08811). D-Wave disputes that these reproduce the full scope — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/d-wave-systematically-rebuts-flatiron-claims-reaffirming-beyond-classical-simulation-milestones/) (industry news; abstract/snippet level throughout)
- **Quantinuum H2 digital Ising magnetism** ([arXiv:2503.20870](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20870); retitled across versions; author order differs between sources): over 2000 two-qubit gates at 99.94(1)% partial-entangler fidelity. They observe Floquet prethermalization "on timescales where accurate simulations using current classical methods are extremely challenging" and extract **diffusion constants** as evidence of local equilibration — [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/arXiv:2503.20870) (abstract level). A related BP-TN study claims classical results with absolute observable accuracy below 0.01, better than the QPU — [arXiv:2508.15759](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15759) "Evaluating classical simulations with a quantum processor" (snippet level; the mapping of that snippet to the paper is uncertain)
- **Google analog-digital simulator** (Andersen et al., Nature 638, 79-85 (2025); [arXiv:2405.17385](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.17385)): 69 transmons, U(1)-symmetric XY analog evolution. Analog performance "exceeds the simulation capacity of known classical algorithms at full system size" by cross-entropy benchmarking. The paper reports Kibble-Zurek breakdown via coarsening, KT signatures, ETH tests in targeted parts of the spectrum, and imaging of energy and vorticity transport during thermalization — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08460-3) (abstract level)
- A 2025 comparative study of 2D TFIM quench dynamics benchmarks MPS, TTN, neural quantum states and 2D TNs on **local magnetization and connected correlations**, finding quantitative discrepancies — [arXiv:2511.19340](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19340) (abstract level)
- A 2026 review, "Quantum Advantage: a Tensor Network Perspective", argues that 2D-native (BP) methods scale far better than 1D MPS on 2D/3D lattices — [arXiv:2603.18825](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.18825) (abstract level)
- Hydrodynamics benchmarks: TEBD converges only to t ≈ 20/J in the infinite-temperature spin model, while DMT and OST reach t = 60/J — [arXiv:2310.06886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06886). KPZ superdiffusion in a quantum gas microscope is an experiment whose comparison relies on local spin transport — [arXiv:2107.00038](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.00038) (title level)

### Inferences
Ranking by fit with our measured regime (non-integrable Ising-type, growth phase, local observables, equal χ):
1. **Quench benchmarks of Ising-type models on quasi-1D or MPS-snake geometries** (IBM kicked Ising beyond 5 steps, D-Wave square/cubic, 2D TFIM comparative study 2511.19340). The headline metrics are exactly 1-site and 2-site observables during entanglement growth, the regime where we gain. A 2-5x error reduction at fixed χ converts into a χ reduction for a target error, and because MPS cost scales as χ³, the "MPS cost to match QPU" estimates such as D-Wave's would shift substantially. *Benefit: high relevance; prior-art risk: moderate* (BP-TN groups compete via geometry rather than truncation rule). Caveat: these are 2D/3D, and MPS snakes make "local" observables non-local along the chain, so the region around a cut is not the region being measured. A TTN/PEPS version of the tilt (PyTreeNet is a tree-TN code) is the natural extension.
2. **Floquet prethermalization / digital Ising (Quantinuum)**: non-integrable Ising, local observables, long times. Fit is good during growth, but extracting diffusion constants needs late times, where DMT/DAOE-type operator methods dominate.
3. **Hydrodynamics / thermalization (diffusion constants, energy transport, Google XY thermalization)**: the targets are local, but U(1)/XY-type models resemble the Heisenberg case where our method fails, and the late-time regime belongs to DMT, rTEBD, DAOE and OST. *Fit: weak, unless the Heisenberg failure is fixed (e.g., by adding conserved currents to the target set, as DMT does).*
4. **ZNE / error-mitigation verification beyond the verifiable regime** (Anand et al. 2306.17839-type MPS baselines): local observables, moderate depth, MPS-based. A cheaper accurate local MPS directly widens the classically checkable window. *Fit: good; prior art: I found none for truncation-rule changes in this context.*
- What the method would *not* change: claims settled by geometry (BP vs MPS on 2D/3D lattices) or by entanglement-agnostic methods (t-VMC, Pauli propagation). Fidelity/XEB-based claims (Google 2405.17385 analog XEB) are untouched, since the method leaves fidelity unchanged.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve per-paper quantitative MPS error-versus-χ curves for local observables in these benchmarks. Those numbers are needed to turn "2-5x" into "χ reduced by factor X".
- I did not verify Google's 2025 OTOC "quantum echoes" paper, IBM's 2025 follow-ups, or the Anand et al. abstract.
